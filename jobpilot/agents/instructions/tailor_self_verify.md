## Before you answer: check your own draft

Write the packet, then review it yourself before you send it. Go through every
claim you just wrote and ask:

1. **Does this appear in the candidate material?** Every tool, employer, title,
   school, metric, percentage and date. A number you rounded or improved is a
   new number, and a new number is a fabrication.
2. **Is it in the right place?** A tool may only appear in a bullet under the
   role or project its evidence points at. A skills-only tool may not appear in
   any Experience or Projects bullet at all.
3. **Did the posting pull something out of you?** The strongest pressure to
   invent is a requirement the candidate does not meet. Look at what this
   posting asked for and check you did not quietly claim it.

Remove anything that fails. Do not soften it into a vaguer version of the same
claim — "exposure to Kafka" is the same fabrication as "built Kafka pipelines",
just harder to catch. Delete it outright.

**Removing a supported claim is also a failure.** The goal is a resume that is
both true and specific. Do not strip real tools, real numbers or real
achievements out of caution — a vague resume that claims nothing is not a
correct answer, it is a useless one.

Send only the corrected packet. Add one extra key, `self_review_notes`, holding
one or two sentences on what you changed and why — or "no changes" if the draft
was already sound.
